03

The CPU burn was a watch waking itself

installed
The triage guessed that watch cost grows with backlog. Profiling showed a 10,000-message store costs about 1.3 s per full scan: real, but nowhere near a whole core. The actual cause was a loop that only happens on Linux. A participant with no inbox folder had its watch anchored on its own participant folder, where the watch writes its heartbeat. The heartbeat woke the scan, the scan read the cursor file, Linux reported the read as a new event, and the watch went round again with no pause. macOS never reports reads, which is why the Mac never showed it.

Shipped

  • A wake made only of reads and the watch's own writes keeps waiting (fd16003)
  • A wait always stops at its deadline (81c0f40)
  • The supervisor ignores participant-file churn as a hint to rescan
  • Scan profiling: POST_WATCH_PROFILE=1 (2443a9a)

How we know

  • Same probe, throwaway store on the devbox, 15 s of watching: the installed build used 0 CPU-seconds and rewrote its heartbeat 4 times; the old build used 2.49 CPU-seconds and rewrote it 3,738 times
  • The live watch that burned 93% of a core for 16 hours was restarted by its owner on the fixed build: 0.51 CPU-seconds per 30 s, about 1.7%
  • Devbox supervisor scans dropped from about 223 a minute to about 5, at 0.9% CPU

A bad cursor file can't pass for new mail

installed
A cursor file that can't be read now labels the batch [cursor unusable: re-reporting history] instead of announcing "339 new" (94579be, landed before the triage). What the renames leave behind

A session that was already running in one of the renamed repos keeps the room name it picked up at launch. Until it restarts, it sends and reads as the old name, and replies go to the other machine's room. New launches use the new name. On the devbox, owner-signed messages already sent as trey no longer show the signed badge, because the badge matches the current owner room and history isn't rewritten. They show as plain messages, not as forgeries.
